## Appendix A — Interview guides

### A.0 How we ran every interview

- **Length:** 15–25 minutes. Choose 4–5 main questions per interview; you do not need to ask every probe.
- **Ask about real past events.** Start with "Tell me about the last time…" rather than "Would you…?"
- **Never ask about features.** Do not ask "Would a matching feature help you?" or "Would you use an app that…?" If a participant suggests a feature, ask *"What problem would that solve for you? When did you last run into it?"*
- **Use neutral follow-ups:** "Can you tell me more?", "What happened next?", "How did that affect you?", "How often does that happen?", "What do you do about it today?"
- **Capture for each pain point:** what happened, how often, what it cost (time, a missed opportunity, a decision), and the current workaround.
- **Two people per interview where possible:** one asks questions, one takes notes and keeps time.

### A.1 Opening and consent script (read it out after recording starts)

> "Thank you for joining. We are Team 050 from the IITM BS Software Engineering course. We are studying how students get career guidance and how alumni and the programme support them. There are no right or wrong answers. We want to hear about your own experiences.
>
> With your permission, we would like to record this conversation. The recording will be used only for our coursework: our team, course instructors and peer reviewers may see a short excerpt. We will not use your name in our report; you will be identified only by a participant ID. You can skip any question or stop at any time.
>
> **Do you agree to this conversation being recorded and to a short excerpt being used as evidence for our course submission?**"

*Wait for a clear spoken "yes" on the recording. If the participant declines, do not record; take written notes only and mark "Consent recorded: No" in the evidence log.*

**Warm-up (1 minute):** "Could you briefly tell us where you are in the programme (or your career) right now?"

### A.2 Guide A — Students (primary users)

*Goal: understand how students make career decisions, find guidance, and follow up on it.*

| # | Main question | Probes | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Can you describe how you are currently deciding what to do after your IITM BS degree? | Which options are you considering? What is hardest about the decision? What information are you still missing? | Pathway, uncertainties, information gaps |
| S2 | Tell me about the last time you needed advice about your career, higher studies or a startup idea. What did you do? | Whom did you approach? How did you find or contact them? How long did it take to get useful guidance? | Channels (LinkedIn, Discord, WhatsApp, seniors), effort, delays |
| S3 | Have you ever struggled to find someone with relevant experience to guide you? What happened? | What made the search hard? What else did you try? What happened because you could not find the right person? | Barriers, workarounds, consequences |
| S4 | When you get career advice, how do you decide whether it applies to your situation? | What makes you trust or doubt it? Have you had advice that did not fit you? | Trust signals, relevance criteria, mismatches |
| S5 | Have you been guided by a senior, a graduate or a working professional? Walk me through that experience. | How did it start? What worked, and what did not? Did the conversation continue afterwards? | What already works, friction, why it stopped |
| S6 | Think about the last guidance you received. What did you do afterwards, and what got in the way? | Did you follow up? How did you keep track of next steps? Did it lead to a concrete action? | Follow-up, drop-off, outcomes |

**Closing:** "Is there anything about getting career guidance that we did not ask about but you think is important?"

### A.3 Guide B — Alumni and recent graduates (secondary users)

*Goal: understand how alumni give guidance, judge requests, manage their time, and keep mentoring relationships going.*

| # | Main question | Probes | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Can you tell me about your path after the IITM BS programme and the big decisions you made along the way? | Which decisions were hardest? What helped? What do you wish you had known earlier? | Areas of expertise they could guide in |
| M2 | Tell me about the last time a student approached you for career advice. How did it happen? | How did they reach you? What did they need? What did you need to know before replying? | Contact channels, context in the first message, effort |
| M3 | What makes it hard to find time to help students? | How do you balance it with work or study? Have you had to postpone or decline? What happened then? | Capacity limits, scheduling friction, reasons for declining |
| M4 | When a student asks for guidance, how do you work out their background, goals and expectations? | What is usually missing? Can you recall when expectations were unclear? How did you clarify them? | Information gaps, wasted back-and-forth |
| M5 | Describe a mentoring interaction that went on over several conversations. What kept it going, or what stopped it? | How were follow-ups arranged? How did you track commitments? Why did it pause or end? | Relationship lifecycle, drop-off points |
| M6 | Have you ever declined a request or felt unable to help a student well? What led to that? | Was it time, expertise, expectations, or something else? What did you tell the student? What happened next? | Rejection reasons, redirection, escalation needs |

**Closing:** "Is there anything about helping students that we have not covered?"

### A.4 Guide C — IIC programme administrators (primary operational users)

*Goal: understand how mentoring and career support are coordinated today, where it breaks down, and what the programme needs to measure.*

| # | Main question | Probes | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Can you walk us through how students currently get career guidance or mentoring through the programme? | Who is involved? What is formal and what is informal? What tools or channels are used? | Current process, actors, tools |
| A2 | What career-related concerns do students bring to the programme most often? | Are some career paths harder to support? Are some student groups harder to serve? How do you respond now? | Demand categories, unmet needs |
| A3 | How does the programme find, contact and coordinate with alumni who are willing to guide students? | How is alumni information kept up to date? How do you know whether someone is still available? What makes engagement hard? | Data upkeep, availability, outreach barriers |
| A4 | Can you describe a recent case where arranging guidance for a student was difficult? | Where were the delays or communication gaps? How was it resolved? How often does this happen? | Specific failures, frequency, effort |
| A5 | How do you currently tell whether students are getting useful guidance and whether interactions are actually happening? | What information do you collect? How are pending or unresolved cases tracked? What is hard to measure? | Current metrics, reporting gaps |
| A6 | What rules or concerns do you consider when connecting students with alumni? | What privacy or consent rules apply? Are there eligibility or safeguarding policies? How are complaints handled? | Policy constraints, escalation paths |

**Closing:** "If this programme were working really well a year from now, what would you see that you do not see today?" *(This asks about outcomes, not features.)*
