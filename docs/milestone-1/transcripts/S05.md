**Interviewer:** Hello, thank you for taking your time to participate in this interview. We are conducting this interview as part of our Software Engineering project at IIT Madras. Our aim is to understand how students make career decisions, find guidance from seniors and overcome challenges related to internships, higher study and career planning. Shall we begin?

**S05:** Yeah, sure.

**Interviewer:** Q1. What are your current career plans after completing the IITM BS degree, and how are you deciding which career path to follow?

**S05:** Honestly speaking, if you had asked this question before the diploma, at the very beginning of the foundation level when I joined this degree, I was very clear. When I heard about data science and applications, I thought I would be a data scientist or data analyst. But as I reached the end of the diploma, I got to know that there are different fields: data scientist, data analyst, data engineer, and so on.

**Interviewer (follow-up):** Are you confused between different career options such as data science and data engineering?

**S05:** Yeah, as of now.

**Interviewer:** Q2. What difficulties are you facing while deciding which skills or technologies to learn?

**S05:** To be very honest, one of the biggest difficulties is that when I talk to my colleagues or others in the degree with me, I realise I really want to talk to someone who is already working as an intern, already working, pursuing a master's, or doing a job. That would give me a better understanding of what the industry actually requires from students, and the key insights related to that.

**Interviewer:** Q3. Can you describe the last time you needed career advice, or any guidance you received before from seniors?

**S05:** When I tried to approach certain problems, I surfed the Internet but never thought to interact with seniors, because there is no dedicated platform to meet or interact with them.

**Interviewer (follow-up):** So you really find it difficult to find seniors or alumni?

**S05:** Yeah, yeah.

**Interviewer:** Q4. When looking for guidance, how important is it that the mentor has experience in your specific career field or the same goals?

**S05:** In future, if I want to interact with seniors, I would like to interact with someone who is already in the field I want to go into. In the beginning I was totally blank, even unaware of the fields and the opportunities we can get after completing the degree. As I approach the later stages of the degree, I am getting to know that there are several different fields, and when I approach seniors, their advice relates to different fields.

**S05:** So I would like to approach someone who is already in the field I am willing to go into.

**Interviewer:** Q5. When you receive career guidance from someone, how do you determine whether their advice is trustworthy and suitable for your situation?

**S05:** As I answered before, there are several different fields, and seniors advise for very specific ones. In the diploma there are two paths: Diploma in Data Science and Diploma in Programming. Accordingly, there are internships for data science and for programming. Career guidance totally depends on the context of which field you are talking about: programming and software development, machine learning, data analysis, data engineering, and so on. So it depends on the field you choose and the type of guidance the seniors give.

**Interviewer:** Q6. Have you ever followed someone's career advice and later found it useful or unsuitable?

**S05:** Yes. Once I joined a career-counselling meeting where a senior who had passed the degree advised going for extracurricular activities in fields not included in our degree courses, such as cloud computing. There are certifications for these, for example from Microsoft or Google, so I started giving such platforms priority.

**S05:** Later I found that continuing the degree with three to four subjects is already quite difficult to manage, and the certifications are not that worthwhile if you are not doing well in your degree subjects. If you have a certificate from Amazon or Google but a CGPA of around 5 or 6, it won't have a very good impact on your CV.

**Interviewer (follow-up):** Was this mentor a senior student, an IITM graduate, or a working professional?

**S05:** He was a graduate, also from IIT, and was currently working in an NGO or startup.

**Interviewer (follow-up):** How did you connect with that person?

**S05:** It was one of the house meetings I joined (we have corporate houses). I was not very aware of that person beforehand; in the session he mentioned that he was working for an NGO or startup.

**Interviewer (follow-up):** Did you improve after that mentorship?

**S05:** I tried to focus on other things not taught in this degree so far, such as cloud computing certifications. But later I found it may not be very useful if you cannot manage all your subjects with a good CGPA.

**Interviewer:** Q7. If someone advises you as per your goals and situation, how do you keep track of your progress and know you are doing better because of their career advice?

**S05:** I have not thought about it in this way, but if this became a dedicated thing, I would be very curious about it. If IIT starts something, there should be a dedicated app, like the dedicated app for coding practice, where we can interact with seniors who are currently doing internships or have passed out and are working full time.

**Interviewer (follow-up):** So if you got tasks from the mentor, you could work on them and know whether the advice is useful or not?

**S05:** Somehow we can say that. If we get a chance to interact with someone who is currently in the industry, it really matters: which fields students should focus on, which skills they should have, what kind of CGPA they need, how they should approach their subjects and how they should manage their time. That would be very helpful.

**Interviewer:** Q8. Based on your experience, what would make it easier for students like you to receive useful career guidance from IIT seniors, graduates or professionals?

**S05:** My only suggestion is an application or platform that directly connects students who are in the first or second year, or the middle of their degree, with someone who is currently working, even for half an hour. They could get a clear understanding of what they need to focus on. For every subject we have a dedicated place to discuss, TA sessions and many other things, and we have IIC support, but that is for students in the final year or doing training. There is nothing like this for students who are just beginning the degree or have just started the diploma.

**S05:** The diploma is much more difficult, and students without a coding background are not aware of the programming languages, so they struggle to clear concepts. There are also two different domains in the diploma, programming and data science, and students face difficulty choosing based on the opportunities available: whether to go for machine learning, software development, or something else. Things are changing rapidly. New tools arrive very often, sometimes every week. People say coding is dead, but something still remains, such as maintaining code and applications, and many new fields have arrived along with AI. Students surfing the Internet read things like "jobs are dead" and get confused.

**S05:** My honest suggestion is a platform where students connect to seniors who are in the industry, so they get clear feedback and insight into what they actually need for their specific journey, whether software development, coding, data science or data analysis. With a clearer, more dedicated pathway and guidance, they won't be distracted or diverted to other fields.

**Interviewer:** Thank you for sharing your experience and opinions. Your responses really helped us understand the challenges and difficulties students are facing. Maybe we can build better things by hearing you. Thank you again for your time and participation.

**S05:** You're most welcome. Can we end the session?

**Interviewer:** Yeah.

### Interviewer notes

- **Pain points described from experience:** started the degree aiming to be a data scientist or analyst, and by the end of the Diploma is unsure which of several data roles to pursue; has never talked to seniors about career questions because there is "no dedicated platform to meet or interact with them", so relies on Internet searches; seniors' advice relates to their own field, which may not be the one the student wants; followed a graduate's advice (met at a house meeting) to take cloud certifications, then found it hard to manage alongside degree courses and of little value with a low CGPA; online claims such as "coding is dead" add confusion.
- **What would have helped:** hearing from people currently interning or working about which skills to focus on, what CGPA is needed, how to approach subjects and how to manage time.
- **Feature suggestions (treated as pointers, not evidence):** a dedicated app or platform connecting students with seniors and working professionals, even for half-hour sessions; a clearer pathway for each field.
- **Conflicts with the client (A01):** S05 wants support from the first or second year, but the client limits the programme to students past Diploma level (open question 1 in Section 5.3 of the report). S05 notes that IIC support today reaches final-year students and those in training.
