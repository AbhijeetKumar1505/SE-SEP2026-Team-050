**Interviewer:** Hi, good evening, S04. Thank you for joining us today. We are conducting a user interview as part of our Software Engineering project. Our project focuses on students who are making important academic and career decisions and may need guidance from seniors, recent graduates, and alumni. We want to understand your experiences, challenges, and expectations. There are no right or wrong answers, so please share your thoughts openly.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 1: Career plans and future goals]

**S04:** I am interested in becoming a data engineer, but I am still unsure whether it is the right career path for me. I have heard that data engineering involves databases, data pipelines, and working with large amounts of data, but I do not fully understand what the day-to-day work looks like. I want to learn which skills are required, what projects I should build, and how to prepare for internships or jobs. Since I am still at the diploma level of the programme, I feel I have time to explore, but I want to use that time in the right way.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 2: Seeking advice from seniors]

**S04:** I have looked for guidance through online resources, videos, LinkedIn posts, and conversations with other students. However, different people recommend different things. Some suggest starting with Python and SQL, while others talk about cloud platforms, big-data tools, or certifications. I would prefer to speak with someone who already works as a data engineer and can explain what is genuinely useful. I do not personally know many people in that role, so finding someone with relevant experience is difficult.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 3: Difficulty finding a relevant mentor]

**S04:** It can be hard to find the right person. I may find someone working in data science, but that does not necessarily mean they have practical experience in data engineering. I am also not always sure how to approach professionals on LinkedIn or whether they will respond. It would be easier if a platform let me search for mentors based on their specific role, skills, projects, education, and work experience.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 4: Confusion about the data engineering career path]

**S04:** I am unsure whether data engineering is suitable for my current background and skill level. I do not know which technologies I should prioritise as a beginner or in what order I should learn them. I am also considering related fields such as data analytics, data science, and software development. I do not want to spend months learning tools without understanding whether they fit my goals. Speaking to someone who has entered the field could help me make a more informed decision.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 5: Concerns about an online IIT degree]

**S04:** One concern is whether an online IIT degree will be accepted by employers. IIT has a strong reputation, but I am not sure whether companies evaluate online-programme students in the same way as students who study on campus. I wonder whether the mode of study could affect my chances of getting shortlisted, securing internships, or finding my first data engineering job. I would like to hear from graduates with similar educational backgrounds who have applied for jobs or internships, rather than relying only on different opinions online.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 6: Evaluating advice from a mentor]

**S04:** I would consider the mentor’s relevant experience and whether they have worked in the field I am interested in. For data engineering, I would prefer someone with practical experience rather than only theoretical knowledge. I would also want the advice to match my current level. Someone experienced might recommend advanced tools, but I need to know what to learn first. I would compare the advice with reliable sources and appreciate explanations of why a particular skill or project matters.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 7: Previous mentorship and guidance experiences]

**S04:** I have used online resources and spoken with other students, but I have not had a structured mentorship experience in which someone helped me plan my career step by step. Much of the advice is general, such as learning Python or SQL, and I still have questions about the order in which to learn skills and how to apply them in real projects. Having someone who understands my situation and can guide me over time would be more useful than a single conversation.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 8: Following up and tracking progress]

**S04:** After receiving advice, I usually search for the recommended resources and try to understand what I should do next. However, information from different sources can be difficult to organise, and I am not always sure whether I am following the right sequence. A clear roadmap with milestones—such as learning fundamentals, completing projects, preparing for internships, and applying for jobs—would help. Regular follow-ups could help me stay focused and ask questions when I get stuck.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 9: Expected features of a career guidance platform]

**S04:** The most important feature would be the ability to find a mentor based on a specific career interest. I would like to connect with data engineers or people with relevant internship, project, or hiring experience. Filters for skills, professional experience, education, and areas of expertise would be useful. I would also value a structured career roadmap, the ability to ask questions, schedule follow-up discussions, and keep track of guidance and next steps.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 10: Trust, accessibility, and possible concerns]

**S04:** I would feel more comfortable if mentor profiles showed their education, professional experience, skills, and relevant projects. A clear way to request mentorship would also make it less intimidating to approach professionals. Mentor availability matters too; if people do not respond, students may lose interest. It would be helpful to know whether sessions are free or paid, because students may have different budgets. The platform should be simple, transparent, and accessible.

**Interviewer:** [Topic 11: Feedback on the project]

**S04:** I think the idea is useful because many students feel confused about their future and may not have professional exposure yet. There is a lot of information online, but it can be difficult to judge what is reliable and relevant to an individual student. Connecting students with people who have faced similar decisions could help. I especially like the idea of finding mentors in specific fields rather than only receiving general career advice. Follow-up conversations and progress tracking could make the platform more useful.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, S04, for your time and for sharing your thoughts. Your feedback helps us understand the challenges students face when choosing a career, evaluating their educational qualifications, finding relevant mentors, and planning their next steps. We will consider these insights while identifying the key features and requirements for our platform.

**S04:** Thank you. I think this is a useful initiative, and I hope it helps students who are unsure about their career options.

**Interviewer:** Thank you again for participating. Have a great evening.

**S04:** Thank you. You too.

### Interviewer notes

- **Pain points described from experience:** unsure whether data engineering suits them and what the day-to-day work involves; advice from online resources, LinkedIn and other students conflicts (Python and SQL, cloud platforms, big-data tools, certifications); nobody in their network works in the role, and someone "working in data science" may not know data engineering; unsure how to approach professionals on LinkedIn or whether they will reply; most advice is general and does not say what to learn first or in what order; information from different sources is hard to organise into a sequence.
- **Concern raised under a topic the interviewer introduced (anticipated, not experienced):** whether employers value an online degree; wants to hear from graduates with the same background.
- **How they judge advice:** the mentor's practical experience in the role, whether it fits their current level, and cross-checks with reliable sources.
- **Feature suggestions (treated as pointers, not evidence):** search and filters by role, skills and education; a career roadmap with milestones; scheduled follow-ups; tracking guidance and next steps; mentor profiles showing education, experience and projects; clarity on whether sessions are free or paid.
- **Contradicted by client (A01):** students searching for mentors (IIC assigns every mentor); paid sessions (volunteer programme, no payments).
- **Eligibility:** S04 is at Diploma level, and the client described the programme as "only for diploma passout" (open question 1 in Section 5.3 of the report). The interview is included because the experience of looking for guidance is the same.
